# JSON at GB/s with the Strictness Kept: State-of-the-Art Plan (revision of the tape-gbps plan)

> **For agentic workers:** REQUIRED SUB-SKILL: Use superpowers:subagent-driven-development (recommended) or superpowers:executing-plans to implement this plan task-by-task. Steps use checkbox (`- [ ]`) syntax for tracking. Record rulings in `docs/superpowers/plans/2026-11-07-json-sota-ledger.md` (create it at Task 1). Every number below is a measured ratio from `benchmarks/results/json-sota-probe-ce7399f/` or a labelled hypothesis; acceptance is by the gates, never by a single absolute.

**Goal:** GB/s on unclemax for every JSON use case — validate-only, retained documents, small documents, NDJSON streams, typed plans and the Java consumers — with duplicate keys rejected after escape decoding, exact number tokens, strict UTF-8 and nothing published before the whole document passes.

**What changes against `2026-11-07-tape-gbps.md`:** the representation stays (the compact tape, §3 there; Task 3's Java readers continue unchanged), the **producer changes**. simdjson On-Demand is replaced by our own stage 2 over simdjson's stage-1 structural index, with a hidden-class style **shape cache** answering the duplicate check and writing directories by permutation, **directories only for documents that will be read by key**, arrays presized from the structural count with capacity retained by the session, and ISA dispatch of our own kernels. The tape-gbps plan's Task 9 (a gated spike) is promoted to the main line because its gate is met: the probe's stage 2 is **1.50–1.55 × the shipped decoder on 4 KB and up and 1.39 × small** (gate: ≥ 1.25 × / ≥ 1.1 ×), producing bit-identical tapes over the whole differential corpus.

**Baseline:** `ce7399f` on `feat/perf-v4` (tape-gbps Tasks 1–2 done; Task 3 in flight). Measurements: `benchmarks/results/json-sota-probe-ce7399f/` (this plan), `tape-decoder-8447a53/`, `tape-gbps-probe-7f55043/`, unclemax Java cells in `json-v4-86a691a/`.

**Scope held from the tape-gbps plan:** its Global Constraints, Review Focus, §3 layout, §4 reads, §5 Myna interchange, §6 allocation and §7 YAML stand. PGO stays out of the repository (user ruling); nothing here depends on profiled images.

## Global Constraints (additions)

- **Strictness is unchanged and now pinned differentially:** the stage-2 producer must accept exactly what the On-Demand decoder accepts over the 200-document corpus and the 60 malformed documents, with bit-identical words, pool and directories (`decoder_test.cc` keeps the On-Demand decoder as the oracle until Task 6 retires it to a test-only header).
- **Public simdjson surface:** stage 1 is reached through `dom::parser::implementation` (a public `std::unique_ptr<internal::dom_parser_implementation>`) and its public members `stage1()`, `structural_indexes`, `n_structural_indexes`; number typing through `simdjson::builtin::numberparsing::parse_number` (the DOM stage-2 number parser, visible in every TU that includes `simdjson.h`). Both are pinned at simdjson 5.1.1 with a static assertion on `SIMDJSON_VERSION` and a differential test, and both need the user's acceptance (§7 Q1); `fast_float` plus our own integer typing is the fallback if refused (tape-gbps Q5).
- **ISA:** the shipped natives stay runnable on baseline x86-64 (`-march=compatibility` images). Any wider kernel is reached by runtime dispatch (`__builtin_cpu_supports`), compiled with target attributes in the same TU, as simdjson's stage 1 is; nothing requires `-march` at build time.
- **Memory:** a borrowed sink document is its padded input plus words (≤ input + 2 words) plus pool (≤ input); a retained document adds 8 B per member of directories. Session capacity retained between documents is bounded by `maxInputBytes` and freed above `retained_input_limit`, as the input buffer is today.

## Review Focus

1. **The shape cache must never skip a real duplicate.** A template hit requires every key to equal the template's bytes at its position *and* the counts to agree; a template is installed only after its object passed the sorted check. Pinned by `shape_cache_rejects_duplicates` (the same key sequence as the template plus one repeated key at every position, at 2–32 keys; a template of N keys against an object of N+1 whose extra key duplicates an earlier one; keys over 32 bytes; escaped spellings of template keys; a different depth with the same keys; the 17th key equal to the 1st after a hit on the 16 before).
2. **Directories by permutation must equal sorted directories.** Pinned by the differential corpus (bit-identical directories) and by `permuted_directories_match_sorted` over objects whose values change the key word offsets between hits (owned strings, nested containers, wide numbers).
3. **Token spans from the index are exact.** A number's span ends at the next structural index minus trailing whitespace and the parser must have stopped exactly there; an escaped string's raw span ends at its closing quote. Pinned by `spans_are_exact_tokens` (kept) and `numbers_followed_by_whitespace`.
4. **Stage-2 grammar is complete:** empty input, every literal with a trailing non-terminator (`truex`), numbers with trailing garbage (`1x`), lone surrogates, bad escapes, unterminated strings and containers, commas and colons in every wrong position, two roots, depth 129, NDJSON boundaries. The 60 malformed documents cover most; Task 1 adds the rest.
5. **A document without directories reads correctly.** `TapeOwner.find` takes the linear path when the directory index is 0 and `validate()` accepts it under `TD_TAPE_NO_DIRECTORIES`; snapshots and `toMyna()` of such documents carry no directory. Pinned in `TapeOwnerTest` and `ctape_test`.

### 1.2 Readings

- **The producer is the problem, not the tape.** `S3` writes the *same words* as `V5` (bit-identical over the 200-document corpus and the four fixtures; both reject all 60 malformed documents) 1.50–1.55 × faster on 4 KB and up and 1.39 × faster on 210 B. The On-Demand walk spends ≈ 0.5 of our stage 2's time on itself: `simdjson_result` wrappers, `type()`, `raw_json_token()`/`trim()`, the iterator's depth bookkeeping, `get_number()` re-reading the token, `get_string()` through the builtin implementation.
- **The builtin implementation is `fallback` in every build the repository makes.** No `-march` is passed, so every On-Demand kernel (string unescaping, number parsing) is the scalar fallback while stage 1 dispatches to `icelake`; `-march=x86-64-v3` does not change that (haswell needs PCLMUL). Our stage 2 is not bound to the builtin: its kernels are ours and dispatch at runtime (§3.4).
- **The duplicate check is nearly free once the shape is cached and the hit path is lean.** With a per-depth template, a hit is one 16-byte SIMD compare and nothing pushed (the hashes and bytes come from the template; a miss materializes them). Validate-only with the check (`S3v`) runs 1.25 × *faster* than the On-Demand walk without any check (`W2c`); on the sink tape the check costs 10–17 % (`S3n/S3x` 0.83–0.90: 2.5–3 ns per key, the remaining target of Task 1's micro-work). Within the On-Demand walk the same cache is worth 7–12 % retained and 19–22 % validate-only (`V5s`, `V5sv`): the cheapest shippable step, independent of the stage 2. On the fixture every object after the first two is a hit (steady state 40,000 hits, 0 sorts on huge); the first document of a new shape (`S3h`) costs ≤ 1 % on big, 23 % on a 210 B document (templates therefore persist across a session's documents).
- **Directories cost 4–6 % of a decode and 8 B per member**, and sink consumers (JSON.parse, `JavaSink`, `TruffleSink`, streams, export) never read them. They become opt-in (§2).
- **Presizing and retained capacity are worth 4–7 % on 4 KB and up and 10 % on small** (`S3/S3k`): the production hint (`input / 3 + 8` words) grows the arrays once or twice per large document, every growth above 32 KB is a fresh upstream allocation (page faults on a 7 MB word array per decode), and on small documents the per-decode reservation of four arrays is a fixed cost. Bounds from the structural count (`2 × n + 2` words, `input` pool bytes, `n / 2 + 1` entries) are exact, and a session that keeps its arrays between documents reserves nothing per decode.
- **NDJSON gains the most**: one stage 1 per batch plus our stage 2 per document is 1.58 × the `iterate_many` + On-Demand path (1.68 × without directories), with document boundaries read from the index (the root closes) instead of a per-document `document_reference`.
- **What remains against simdjson's DOM** (`S3/D0` = 0.59): `D0` copies every string but checks nothing, keeps no spans and runs AVX-512 kernels; `S3x` (no check) is 0.75 × `D0` and `S3v` (no tape) 0.77 ×. The gap is the pool and span bookkeeping, the shape compare, and the fallback number parser (`parse_number`'s eight-digit step is SIMD only in the haswell/icelake namespaces, which the builtin does not reach). Task 7 pursues the kernels; §7 Q5 the number parser.

### 1.3 ISA (round 2: the probe built with `-march=x86-64-v3`)

Within noise of round 1 on every row (`S3` 931 / 963 MB/s big / huge against 941 / 985; `V5` 672 / 708 against 678 / 716): 32-byte string scans do not help a fixture whose strings are 2–60 bytes, and the flag leaves simdjson's builtin at `fallback`, so the On-Demand rows cannot gain from a wider build at all. The ISA lever is therefore ours alone (our stage-2 kernels, dispatched; §3.4), it is worth measuring only on long-string workloads and on unclemax's AVX-512, and it is gated (Task 7), not assumed.

Directories are the one representation change: a borrowed sink document has none (view flag `TD_TAPE_NO_DIRECTORIES`), `validate()` accepts the flag, and the Java miss path (already linear for objects without a directory) serves the rare by-key read on such a document. Lazy directories (built on first read) were considered and rejected: the first read would need mutable native storage behind a boundary call, and the eager permuted write costs 4–6 % only on documents that asked for it.

## 3. Architecture

### 3.1 Stage 2 (`json/native/stage2.h`)

```
stage1:   dom::parser::implementation->stage1(buf, len, mode)      (icelake/haswell/westmere/fallback by simdjson)
stage2:   iterative state machine over structural_indexes[0..n): value / after / key states, an explicit
          frame stack (container word index, count, object?), depth ≤ limits.depth
strings:  find_quote_or_backslash(p): 16/32/64-byte compare + movemask (dispatch, §3.4); clean → string_ref
          (span, ASCII from the OR of high bits); escaped → SIMD-copy unescape into the pool, scalar escapes,
          surrogate pairs validated; raw span = token with quotes
numbers:  token = [idx[at], idx[at+1]) minus trailing whitespace; simdjson::builtin::numberparsing::parse_number
          into a writer that maps s64/u64/double (−0 → F64) and spans; any error → RFC 8259 grammar → DECIMAL
literals: memcmp + terminator check; everything else: syntax error
keys:     writer.key_text(ptr, len) → shape cache (§3.2); then the key word as any string
end:      writer.end(index, count, kind): next, count (saturating), directory by permutation or by sort
```

Stage 1 validates UTF-8 and rejects raw control bytes inside strings for the whole buffer before stage 2 reads a byte; stage 2 reads the padded input only (loads of up to 32 bytes past a token stay within `SIMDJSON_PADDING`).

### 3.2 Shape cache (in `tape/native/ctape.h`'s writer and the `null` writer)

One template per depth (32 depths): up to 32 keys of up to 32 bytes stored as masked 8-byte words, their FNV-1a hashes and the sorted rank of each key. Per key: `len` compare plus one to four masked word compares; on a mismatch the object is marked, the key is hashed, and at close the object's run is sorted and checked as today, then installed as the template (keys are copied into the template, so templates survive across documents in a session and across NDJSON records). At close of a matched object with the template's count: no duplicate (the template passed), directory entries written at `rank[i]` with the template's hash and this object's key offset. Objects of 0–1 members: no run, no directory (unchanged). Keys over 32 bytes or objects over 32 keys: the sorted path, no template.

### 3.3 Session and streams

- `session` keeps a `stage1` parser (`dom::parser` sized to `maxInputBytes`, released above `retained_input_limit` as the input buffer is) and its words/pool/directory/pending arrays between documents (capacity bounded by the largest document seen, released above `retained_input_limit`); a decode presizes from `n_structural_indexes` and never grows. `index_allocations` stays 0 in steady state; the first decode of a larger document allocates once.
- Flags: `TD_JSON_WANT_DIRECTORIES` (new; set by `JsonSession.decode`/`retain` paths and `JsonMessage`, not by `parse(sink)`, streams or JSON.parse), `TD_JSON_VALIDATE_ONLY` (unchanged). The view carries `TD_TAPE_NO_DIRECTORIES` when none were built.
- Streams: stage 1 in `stage1_mode::streaming_partial` over each batch of the carry-over buffer; documents are delimited by the index (a root closes when the frame stack empties); the truncated tail is the bytes from the last complete root's end (`structural_indexes[n_structural_indexes]`, as `document_stream` computes it). The carry-over, backpressure and error contract of `stream.h` is unchanged; `iterate_many` and `document_reference` go.
- `td_json_decode`/`_adopted` (one-shot documents) use the same stage 2 through `json::document`.

### 3.4 ISA dispatch (our kernels only)

`find_quote_or_backslash`, the unescape copy loop and the ASCII fold are compiled three times in one TU under `#pragma clang attribute push(__attribute__((target("avx512bw,avx512vl,bmi2"))))`, `target("avx2,bmi,bmi2,lzcnt,popcnt")` and baseline, selected once per process by `__builtin_cpu_supports` (the stage-2 template is instantiated per kernel set so the whole walk inlines its kernels). simdjson's stage 1 keeps its own dispatch. Round 2 (§1.3) bounds the gain; Task 7 measures each tier on unclemax and keeps a tier only if it is ≥ 3 % faster than the one below on big/huge in the same run.

### 3.5 Typed plans

`typed.h` today walks On-Demand objects and resolves every key through `unordered_map::find`. On stage 2 the plan's writer resolves ordinals through the shape cache: a template position caches `ordinal(key)` so a template hit costs no lookup; a miss resolves and installs. Slot writes, masks, contracts and `H1` (through the session parser) are unchanged. Hypothesis: ingest 1.1–1.3 × (the On-Demand share of the 175 ns/record cell); measured in Task 5, kept only if ≥ 1.1 ×.

## 4. What stops, what continues (in-flight work)

- **Task 3 of the tape-gbps plan (Java readers on the compact tape, legacy Myna path deleted): continue unchanged.** The tape layout, the directory layout and the hit path are untouched here; the stage 2 produces bit-identical words. Two amendments for the worker: (1) do not assume every object of ≥ 2 members has a directory — `find` keeps its linear path for `dir == 0` and the `directories()` diagnostic counts only objects with one; (2) `JsonSession.parse(sink)`, `JsonStream` and the JSON.parse path must not request directories once `TD_JSON_WANT_DIRECTORIES` exists (Task 3 may leave the flag set everywhere; Task 4 here flips the sink paths and measures). Nothing in Task 3 should depend on `simdjson::ondemand` types or on `legacy_decoder.h` (it deletes the latter, as planned).
- **Task 4 (JVM backend) and Task 5 (Myna interchange) of the tape-gbps plan: continue** (representation work, producer-independent).
- **Task 6 (unclemax measurement) of the tape-gbps plan: fold into Task 8 here** (one unclemax pair after the producer lands, not two).
- **Task 7 (text-arena fusion): defer** until Task 8's numbers; the stage 2 can write the text arena during its string pass later at lower cost than the On-Demand walk could.
- **Task 8 (YAML on the compact tape): continue**; the writer's shape cache applies to rapidyaml's handler through `end_rescan` only if measured (not in scope).
- **Task 9 (stage-2 spike): superseded** by Tasks 1–2 here; its gate is met (§0).
- **Do not pursue:** a yyjson-style single-pass parser (stage 1 is 3 % of the decode and gives us UTF-8 validation, control-byte rejection and token boundaries at 16 GB/s; a single pass would re-implement all of it for no measured headroom), DOM plus a post-pass (`D0e` 0.80 GB/s in `tape-gbps-probe-7f55043`, no spans), bloom or perfect-hash duplicate filters (the shape cache makes the common case one compare per key; the sorted run is the uncommon case and already O(n log n) per object), lazy directories (§2).

## 6. Risks and non-goals

- *simdjson's `internal` surface moves.* Pinned at 5.1.1; an upgrade re-runs the differential corpus; if `parse_number` or `stage1` change shape, the fallback is `fast_float` for doubles plus our own integer typing with the same differential test (two days, not a redesign).
- *A shape-cache bug silently accepts a duplicate.* Review Focus 1's tests, the differential corpus, and fuzzing in Task 2 (random key sequences with injected duplicates against a sorted oracle, 10⁶ objects in the test).
- *Retained session capacity grows memory.* Bounded by the largest document below `retained_input_limit` (1 MiB input → ≤ 2 MiB words + 1 MiB pool + 0.5 MiB directory); above it, released as the input buffer is.
- *Streams change failure extents.* Stage-1 errors stay per batch (as documented today); document-level errors keep their extent from the index.
- *RBE noise.* Every gate is a ratio within one run; unclemax pairs decide.

**Non-goals:** replacing simdjson's stage 1 or writing a single-pass parser; a DOM-based decoder; lazy directories; changing `TapeSink`, typed slots, the session/stream public API or the compact tape layout; threading.

## 7. Open decisions for the user

1. **simdjson `internal` surface on the production path:** `dom::parser::implementation->stage1()` / `structural_indexes` and `builtin::numberparsing::parse_number`, pinned at 5.1.1 with a differential oracle (proposed), or `fast_float` + our own integer typing for numbers (a new dependency, our own typing rules to pin)? The stage-1 access is needed either way; it is the same member `document_stream` uses.
2. **Directories for `retain` of a borrowed sink document:** build them at retain time (a rescan: ≈ the cost of one directory pass) or refuse (`TD_JSON_ARGUMENT`; Java's `decode` always requests them)? Proposed: build at retain time.
3. **Shipped ISA tiers:** keep the baseline x86-64 natives and add dispatched AVX2/AVX-512 kernels for *our* stage 2 only (proposed), or also ship an `x86-64-v3` build of the whole library so simdjson's builtin becomes `haswell` (only matters while On-Demand remains, i.e. typed plans if Task 5 fails)?
4. **Typed plans:** move to stage 2 only on ≥ 1.1 × (proposed) or unconditionally to retire On-Demand from production?
5. **Number parser ISA:** `parse_number` through `simdjson::builtin` is scalar; reaching the haswell/icelake variants means instantiating simdjson's generic number parsing under our own target pragmas (fragile) or carrying our own eight-digit SIMD step in Task 7. Proposed: measure in Task 7, decide then.
6. **Template limits:** 32 keys × 32 bytes per depth, 32 depths (proposed; 12 KB per session); larger templates cost memory per session, smaller ones miss on wide records.
